# Imagery Slop — the Generated and Generic Tells

The imagery-specific complement to `design-principles` → ai-slop.md (which covers the general tells, including generic icon packs and stock illustration) — don't restate the philosophy here. This is the checklist you run on **every candidate image, at the size it will actually be seen, in the surface it's for.** List what you find; "none" is a reflex, not a result.

## Generation Artifacts — reject or edit

- **Anatomy errors** — extra or fused fingers, wrong joint counts, mismatched earrings, teeth that merge. Zoom in on hands and faces every time.
- **Garbled text** — letter-like shapes on signs, screens, labels, book spines, clothing. Even "no text" prompts leak it; edit it out.
- **Melted geometry** — objects that fuse into each other, furniture legs that don't reach the floor, impossible reflections, repeating background elements.
- **Waxy skin** — poreless, airbrushed faces with that even plastic sheen.
- **Physically impossible light** — shadows going two directions, a subject lit from a source that isn't in the scene.

## The Generated Look — a style failure, even when nothing is broken

- **HDR glow** — every surface evenly lit, saturated, and sharp edge to edge. Real light falls off.
- **The default model aesthetic** — teal-orange grading, cinematic lens flare, bokeh on everything, a golden-hour glow nobody asked for. It's what the model does when the prompt didn't decide.
- **Dead-center symmetry** — the subject plonked in the middle with nothing to read around it, no room for type.
- **Over-rendered 3D** — glossy clay figures, floating isometric islands, iridescent blobs. The illustration equivalent of the purple gradient.
- **Too perfect** — no dust, no wear, no clutter, no asymmetry. Spec'd honesty ("working hands") delivered as a showroom.

## Stock Clichés — the category default

- **People pointing at laptops, handshakes, headsets, high-fives** — and the staged diverse-team-around-a-table shot.
- **The smile to camera** — unless the spec asked for it, it reads as advertising.
- **Abstract tech** — glowing networks, binary rain, a blue hand touching a hologram.
- **The literal metaphor** — a lightbulb for ideas, a rocket for growth, a chess piece for strategy.

## Off-Spec — the image is fine; it just isn't this brand's

- **Palette drift** — dominant colors outside the spec's hex anchors; the action color appearing where the spec says it can't.
- **Grade mismatch** — the image wasn't run through the treatment, or it was and still sits apart from the set.
- **Wrong light, lens, or crop** for the spec — the most common way a good image fails.
- **Illustration technique drift** — a different line weight, a second perspective, an extra color.
- **Icon stroke or corner mismatch** against the chosen set or against the body type.
- **Never-list violations** — check each item on the class's never list explicitly.

## In Place — the family test

- **Thumbnail test** — does it still read at the size it'll actually be seen (in a feed, in a card)?
- **Type test** — is there room for the headline where the layout needs it, at a contrast the type can sit on?
- **Set test** — next to the approved images, does it look like a sibling? An image that wins on its own and breaks the set is a reject.
- **Cover-the-logo test** — with the brand's mark covered, does the imagery still say *this* brand rather than the category? (See `design-principles` → Distinctiveness Must Survive Application.)
