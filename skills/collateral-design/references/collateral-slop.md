# Collateral Slop — the Assembled-Not-Designed Tells

Patterns that immediately signal a piece of collateral was *assembled from a template* rather than *designed*. This is the collateral-specific complement to `design-principles` → ai-slop.md and `web-design` → web-slop.md — don't restate the general philosophy here; this is the checklist you run on the **rendered artifact, in its real context** (projected, at thumbnail, on paper). List what you actually find; "none" is a reflex, not a result.

## Across All Collateral

- **The template underneath.** You can feel the master: title box, body box, same placement every page. Content sized by default, not by importance.
- **Everything one weight.** No visible primary/secondary/tertiary; nothing is the one thing that matters, so nothing lands.
- **Centered by reflex.** Every headline and block centered because the frame is fixed; pairs drift off the grid, the reading path dies. Left-align by default.
- **The logo as decoration.** Mark stretched, recolored off-system, dropped on a busy field, or blown up huge on every surface instead of a quiet, consistent placement.
- **Accent everywhere.** The one reserved color (the point / the action) sprinkled on every heading, box, and rule, so it stops meaning anything.
- **Clip-art / mismatched icons.** Icons from three different styles, emoji beside line icons, generic clip art standing in for a real visual idea.
- **Stock-photo clichés.** The handshake, the diverse-team-at-laptop, the headset support agent, the lightbulb "idea" — a stock cliché where a real product shot or owned image belongs.
- **A pile of identical templates.** A "family" that's the same layout stamped across deck, sheet, and ad — coherence faked by cloning instead of earned by a shared system with varied layouts.
- **Distinctiveness only in the logo.** Cover the mark and it's anonymous — the system didn't survive application.
- **Type that dies at final size.** Sizes carried over from a screen design into a small physical format — a card's role or contact line landing at 3–4pt, legible only because you reviewed it scaled up. Check the real size (see [viewer.md](viewer.md)).
- **Only ever reviewed at reduced scale.** A shrunken contact sheet hides exactly the defects that matter; if nobody saw the piece at true size, treat it as unreviewed.
- **Text and boxes on an empty ground.** No background element tying the composition together — the single most common reason a fixed-format piece reads flat. (See [composition.md](composition.md).)
- **A flat black print piece.** Near-black stock treated as the default rather than a deliberate exception, with no light expression designed at all. Also: flat `#000` where a gradient with depth belonged.
- **One two-column split, repeated.** The same 2-col band down the whole piece — the fixed-format equivalent of banded web sections.
- **Cramped interior margin.** Screen-sized padding carried onto paper, so content crowds the trim; reads cheap no matter how good the type is.
- **Bleed designed at trim size.** Background art stopping at the cut line instead of extending past it — white slivers on delivery.
- **Social judged in isolation and static.** No platform context and no motion, so nobody saw how it actually lands in a feed.
- **Type sized to the artboard, not the screen.** Social/thumbnail text that reads at 100% on a desktop and is illegible in-feed on a phone — the single most common social failure.
- **A button on a printed piece.** A pill CTA on a flyer or leave-behind, implying a click that can't happen; use a URL, short link, or QR code.
- **A synthetic oblique.** Faked italic on a face with no italic cut — slanted, distorted letterforms; a reliable amateur tell, usually in a pull-quote.
- **Background excess.** Wash *and* rings *and* grid *and* a giant numeral stacked in one frame — the accumulation, not the pattern, is what reads as AI-generated. Also: a motif clashing behind live text instead of yielding to it.
- **A motif politely contained.** Background elements shrunk to fit inside the canvas instead of scaled up and cropped by the frame — timid where off-canvas would create dynamism.
- **Over-corrected margin on small formats.** So much interior margin on a business card that the content looks stranded and the card reads empty.
- **Geometry-and-type only.** No photography or illustration anywhere in the media mix, so "varied layout" is still the same two ingredients rearranged.

## Decks

- **Bullet soup.** Every slide a title + three-to-five bullets; hierarchy flattened list by list.
- **Mode confusion.** A read/send deck built as one-word talk-track slides (useless emailed), or a presented deck with paragraph slides (audience reads, stops listening).
- **Metric-titled data slides.** "Q3 Revenue" instead of the takeaway ("Revenue doubled after launch"); the chart states nothing.
- **Default chart chrome.** Excel/rainbow palettes, gridline clutter, legend chrome the takeaway already carries — charts that don't match the system.
- **No rhythm.** Twenty identical layouts; no section dividers, no varied slide types — coherent and dead.
- **Projector-blind.** Under-scaled type and low-contrast tint-on-tint that read on a laptop and vanish in a bright room.

## Print

- **RGB headed to print.** File in RGB, no CMYK conversion or color check — colors will shift on press.
- **No bleed / no safe margin.** Edge content with no bleed (white slivers) or critical content in the trim zone (sheared off).
- **Low-resolution raster.** 72-DPI screen images placed at print size — soft or pixelated on paper; raster where vector belonged.
- **px instead of physical units.** Document set in pixels, not mm/in at trim size.
- **Registration black for artwork/text**, or a flat 100% K "black" that prints washed grey where a rich black was needed.
- **Fold ignored.** A tri-fold laid out as three flat columns; content broken across a crease; reading order wrong.
- **Screenshot as deliverable.** A web/JPG export handed off instead of a press-ready PDF with bleed, embedded fonts, 300 DPI.

## Ads

- **Doesn't survive the thumbnail.** Message only legible at full size; dies at feed/scroll scale.
- **Competing messages.** Two or three claims fighting for the one second; no single focal point.
- **One master letterboxed** across every placement instead of composed per aspect ratio and size.
- **Text-crammed.** A paragraph where a hook belongs; heavy text that tanks performance.
- **CTA that doesn't pop.** Action color bled into headline/decoration so the button blends; or no clear single CTA.
- **Ignoring UI-safe zones.** Message or mark buried under the stories/reels top-and-bottom UI overlay.
